BiMPay is live: what instant payments mean for your Barbados business

Published March 2026 · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

On June 12, 2026, the Central Bank of Barbados switched on BiMPay, the country's national instant payment system. A payment that used to take hours or days now settles in seconds, at any hour, on any day.

What changed since we first published this

We wrote this in March 2026, when go-live was set for March 31 and we described fee-free payments for small businesses. The Central Bank later moved go-live to June 12, and business fees are now set by each bank or credit union. We've updated the article to match, and linked the Central Bank's own pages at the end.

What BiMPay is

BiMPay is the Central Bank's payment rail for moving money between individuals, businesses and government around the clock. The person paying and the person being paid don't have to use the same bank, and the recipient has the money immediately.

The six commercial banks and the three largest credit unions are connected to it. The Central Bank also offers its own BiMPay e-wallet app, which opened for download from 1:00 a.m. on June 13 and lets people pay using a phone number, email address or QR code.

For merchants: faster money, with a few things to know

Cash flow that doesn't wait for Monday

  • Saturday night sale? The money arrives in seconds.
  • Sunday brunch rush? Every payment settles as it happens.
  • Public holiday? Settlement carries on.

You and the customer both get confirmation, so you know the money has arrived before they leave the counter.

Payments are final

Because settlement is instant, a BiMPay payment can't be reversed. Check the amount before you confirm. If you need to refund a customer, you send a separate payment, and if something goes wrong, contact your bank or payment provider straight away.

What it costs

Individuals pay nothing to send or receive money. For businesses, the Central Bank said no bank or credit union could charge business fees before September 15, 2026. After that date, fees for micro, small and corporate businesses are set by each business's own financial institution. Small businesses under a revenue threshold pay no fees, but the Central Bank hasn't published that threshold in the pages we found. The Governor has said he expects BiMPay to be free or low-cost depending on the size of the business.

That date has passed, so ask your bank what it charges your business today.

Compare it with your card fees

A BB$12.50 sale with a 2.5% card fee costs about BB$0.31 to process. At 100 sales a day, that's about BB$31 a day, or roughly BB$940 over a 30-day month.

2.5% is an example, not a quote. Put in your own card rate and sales count, then set it against your bank's BiMPay rate for your business.

Do you need a bank account?

Your customers don't. Anyone can use the Central Bank's e-wallet instead, though people using only the wallet have limits on what they can receive: BB$750 a day, BB$2,500 a month and BB$30,000 a year.

For businesses, the first phase of the e-wallet is aimed at individuals, and informal micro-merchants can use a personal wallet to receive payments. A separate merchant wallet, with an app for setting prices and viewing transaction reports, is planned for a later phase. Most businesses will want to connect BiMPay through their own bank or credit union.

Your BiMPay questions, answered

When did BiMPay launch?

June 12, 2026, at 11:59 p.m. It was first announced for March 31. The Central Bank moved the date so it could be certain the system worked across every institution.

Is BiMPay better than a card terminal?

For local payments, it can be: faster settlement, no reversals to chase, and potentially lower fees. Whether it's cheaper depends on what your bank charges your business. Cards still have a place, especially for visitors with international cards, since BiMPay handles local payments only at first.

How secure is it?

The rail is owned by the Central Bank, and every connected institution must follow local and international data-protection rules and report breaches to the Central Bank. As with any payment, check the details before you send.

How do I get started?

Start with the Central Bank's BiMPay page, then ask your bank or credit union how to accept BiMPay for your business.

What this means for Barbados businesses

A level playing field

Small businesses get the same instant settlement that larger ones can negotiate for, without paying for expensive payment equipment.

More of your sales kept

If BiMPay costs you less than your card processor, the difference stays in your business. Check your own numbers before you decide.

More customers can pay digitally

With the wallet option, people without a traditional bank account can pay you digitally. That's good for individuals and for the economy.

Getting ready: five steps

  1. Stay informed. Follow the Central Bank's BiMPay page for updates.
  2. Talk to your bank. Ask how to accept BiMPay and what it charges your business.
  3. Run the numbers. Compare your current card fees with your bank's BiMPay rate.
  4. Plan your ordering and checkout. If you use an ordering tool like Butta, ask about BiMPay support and timing.
  5. Brief your team. Make sure staff know payments are final, so they check amounts before confirming.
